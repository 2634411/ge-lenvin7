What to prepare before ordering a website: a checklist for business

Elena · 2026-11-18

To discuss building a website you do not have to learn programming first. It is enough to describe your business, the visitor's action and the materials you already have. This list helps agree the scope of work and compare offers from different developers.

1. Decide why you need a website

Start with one main goal: to receive enquiries about a service, show projects, introduce the company or sell goods. Then name the visitor's action: write on WhatsApp, call, send a request or place an order.

For example: “We repair furniture in Tbilisi. We need a page with examples of our work so that a person can send a photo and ask for a quote.” This is clearer than “make a modern website”.

2. List the pages or blocks

For a single service a one-page site is often enough: offer, what the service includes, examples, how you work and contacts. If there are several directions and each needs details, separate pages with a common menu are more convenient. One page: one service, promotion, event or a specialist's business card. Company website: several services, company information, projects and contacts. Store: catalogue, cart and order checkout; it should be discussed separately.

In Lenvin packages a one-page HTML site includes up to five blocks and costs 75 ₾, and a corporate HTML site with up to five pages of five blocks each costs 190 ₾. Additional functions are chosen separately; the current scope is on the service pages.

3. Gather texts and business details

You need: the name, a short description and the area you work in; services or products (what is included, who they suit, what the limits are); prices or an explanation of what the quote depends on; the procedure for enquiries, doing the work and handing over the result; contacts and company details that must be published.

You do not have to write finished marketing texts right away: you can prepare facts and drafts and agree text preparation as a separate job. Use only testimonials, figures and results that you can confirm.

4. Choose photos, a logo and examples

Put the images in one folder. Label what they show and in which section to use them. If possible, send the original files rather than reduced copies from a chat. Check that you have the right to publish the photos and consent to show client materials.

Add two or three websites whose design you like and write something specific about each: “convenient menu”, “large photos of work”, “a clear list of services”. This helps discuss design concretely.

5. List the functions and check the request path

A WhatsApp button and a form that sends data are different tasks. For each function write down what the person does and what result they should get. Contact: open a chat, call or send a filled-in form. Request: which fields are required, where the message goes and what the sender sees. Catalogue: how many products, which filters and where prices come from. Updating: who changes the materials and whether a dashboard is needed. Integrations: which existing services must be connected.

A basic HTML site is edited in files. If you want to add news yourself through a dashboard, say so before choosing a package.

6. Clarify the domain, hosting and handover

If you already have a domain and hosting, at the first stage it is enough to give their names and say whether a site works now. If you do not, we will discuss hosting. Costs of external services are counted separately.

Agree in advance: which files and instructions you will receive, who publishes the site, how mobile operation is checked, how many rounds of corrections are included and what counts as a new task. Fix the deadline after the scope is defined and the materials are ready.

Example of a short website brief

An illustrative example, not a description of a completed order. Business: furniture repair in Tbilisi. Goal: enquiries with a photo of the furniture and a description of the task. Format: one page, five blocks — offer, services, examples of work, how we work, contacts. Materials: texts in a document, eight photos of own work, no logo. Contact: a WhatsApp button and a phone number; no form, payment or customer account. Hosting: domain and hosting not chosen yet. Updating: materials change rarely, instructions needed. Budget and desired date: to be stated before the quote.

If some item is not yet known, just say so. It is a topic for discussion, not an obstacle to a first enquiry.

Checklist before sending to a developer

  • It is clear who the site is for and what the visitor should do.
  • A list of pages or blocks is made.
  • Facts, contacts and available materials are collected.
  • The functions that are really needed are marked.
  • It is stated whether there is a domain and hosting and who will update the site.
  • Budget and desired launch date are given.
