Which website does your business need: seven questions before choosing

Elena · 2026-11-16

Before discussing design it helps to describe how the site will work. The answers need no technical knowledge: it is enough to explain who will come, what they should do and who will update the materials.

1. Who will come and with what question?

Not just “all customers” but a specific situation: choosing equipment, checking a contractor, finding out repair terms. Write down two or three questions of such a visitor. They will suggest the page content better than a list of nice effects.

2. What action do you want?

Open a chat, send a request, ask for a quote or fill a cart? Name who receives the request and the employee's next step. A contact button and a confirmed form submission are different features.

3. How many different offers need explaining?

One offer may fit on one page. Several services with different terms are easier with separate addresses. Choosing products needs a catalogue. A detailed comparison of the three formats will help define the first version.

4. Who will change the content after launch?

If updates are rare and made by the developer, an HTML site with file changes can be considered. If an employee regularly adds articles or products, a suitable management method is needed. WordPress has a block editor for pages and posts; what you can do on your own site depends on the theme and setup.

Ask to be shown a real operation: replacing a photo or adding a page.

5. What data must pass between systems?

Name the source of prices and stock, the recipient of requests, the delivery and payment you need. For a store this affects the scope more than the number of similar-looking pages.

6. What is ready now and who is responsible for the site afterwards?

List the texts, photos, domain and hosting that are ready, the desired date and the budget. Separately assign updates, backups and support.

7. What does the budget for the first version include?

Separate development, domain and hosting, paid components, later changes and attracting visitors. Choose the functions needed to start. Clarify what can be added later and whether it will require reworking the foundation.

A short example answer

An illustrative situation: “We have one repair service. The visitor should see examples and write to the master. Texts and photos are ready and change twice a year. The domain is not chosen yet. We need file handover and clear instructions.” This is a basis for discussing an HTML page. If a manager will add photos every week without code, the management method should be reconsidered.

Choosing a platform does not replace useful content and technical accessibility: Google does not promise automatic top positions.
